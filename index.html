    Words to know
    • PU (power units): how this game counts electricity.
    • Radiation dose (mSv): how much space radiation a person's body has absorbed. Too much can harm health.
    • Solar flare: a burst of energy from the Sun that can send out harmful radiation.
    • Regolith: loose rock and dust on the Moon or Mars. Piled on a base, it works like a shield.
    • Recycler: a machine that cleans used air and water so the crew can use them again.
    • Fission reactor: a small nuclear power plant that works day and night.
    Which numbers are real?

    Solar storms come from real solar flares recorded in NASA's space weather database (DONKI). Everyday radiation follows real measurements: about 1.4 mSv a day on the Moon (Chang'e-4 lander) and about 0.65 on Mars (Curiosity rover), compared with about 0.5 on the International Space Station. Crew needs are close to real: about 0.84 kg of oxygen and 0.6 kg of food per person each day, and space stations recycle most of their water.

    The game scales some numbers so a mission fits in 12 weeks: power units, cargo units, the 120 mSv mission limit (NASA's career limit is 600 mSv), and how big each storm is.
